# Discovery and ideation

Begin with the user's outcome and meaningful situations: who needs what, under which constraints, and what observable improvement matters. Allow references to generate better ideas, not merely decorate a predetermined solution. Distinguish hard constraints from examples and weak preferences.

For consequential taste choices, compare actual artifacts with comparable content and tasks. The user can reject both options; that can change the selection criteria or problem framing. Update the operation that selects future references, not just a sentence in the brief.

Describe unresolved mechanisms without the domain's vocabulary: retaining progress across failure, finding rare useful evidence, coordinating independent judgments, or preserving identity while adapting. Search direct solutions and analogous structures in other fields. Reserve bounded effort for older and neglected work when another framing could matter. One failed query does not disprove the avenue.

When expertise or the method of working is the gap, include skills, plugins, prompts, expert practice, and their executable support as references. Read [expert-process transfer](expert-processes.md) before extracting or packaging their judgment. Inspect what changes a decision rather than collecting expert personas or installing whole catalogs.

Inspect primary material: actual behavior, implementation, tests, repository history, release/version, license, and benchmark setup. Recency alone is not quality; a current README does not prove a working feature. Preserve the population, filters, baseline, and counting unit behind performance claims. Correlated repositories or derivative articles are not independent confirmations.

Give each reference a job and record the inspected property, uncertainty, and competing explanation. A screenshot supports visible layout, not persistence behavior. Source supports intended logic, not necessarily deployed behavior. Keep multiple contributions per reference and multiple references per contribution.

Before narrowing, inspect important omissions, contrary evidence, and relevant unused sources. Retrieval ranking can systematically hide distant analogies. Keep a neglected-reference queue, with what question would make each worth reopening.

Stop convergent search when further evidence is unlikely to change the next decision. Distinguish that from access failure or budget exhaustion; preserve unresolved questions and the next discriminating probe. No fixed source count establishes completeness. Refresh unstable claims for each project; the method's historical research cutoff does not freeze future discovery.

## Carry research into the producing operation

Before committing to a consequential approach, investigate existing solutions,
underlying mechanisms, likely bottlenecks and alternatives that could remove
unnecessary work. Examine accuracy, runtime, token cost, integration effort and
environment readiness where they affect the goal. Test promising adaptations
with a bounded probe that distinguishes the competing explanations.

When failures recur, locate the supported cause and search structurally similar
problems beyond the immediate domain. Reconsider the objective, route and
evaluator when repeated patches yield no new evidence. Preserve the full goal;
changing an approach does not authorize shrinking the requested outcome.

The workflow planner and context compiler carry `approach_review` into the
implementation handoff. It is a proposed operation, not a claim that research
ran. The agent must apply it, inspect outputs and record the selected mechanism,
conditions, rejected alternatives and discriminating evidence. Reuse valid prior
evidence for settled work instead of requiring research on every small edit.
